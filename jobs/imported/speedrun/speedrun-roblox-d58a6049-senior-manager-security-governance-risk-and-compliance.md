About the role

Roblox is looking for a Senior Manager of Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC) to own how we identify, quantify, and govern security risk across the company. This is a risk-first role: our primary focus is identifying and managing risk, and that focus is what enables us to meet our compliance obligations.

You will lead the GRC team and be accountable for Roblox’s security risk posture, from the methodology we use to rate risk, to the Top Risks we report on, to the decisions leaders make because of what we show them. Our program takes a balanced, “right-sized” approach, combining qualitative and quantitative methods, including Factor Analysis of Information Risk (FAIR), to focus the company on the risks that matter most. We use engineering to automate risk management, supply chain risk, AI risk, and controls monitoring, so that we mitigate risk with code rather than compliance checklists.

You will be a key member of the InfoSec leadership team, helping set security priorities for Roblox. You will own security risk reporting to the Board of Directors’ Audit & Compliance Committee and executive leadership, and work with Engineering, Legal, Privacy, Finance, and the business to reduce risk without slowing Roblox down.

This position is part of the Information Security team. This role will report to the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO).

You will:

- Own Roblox’s security risk management program end to end, including the risk methodology, Top Risks, the risk register, the semi-annual risk refresh, and the risk exception and acceptance process.

- Be accountable for Top Risks: drive mitigation plans with Security, Engineering, and Infrastructure leaders, track progress to target residual risk, and escalate when risk falls outside tolerance.

- Own security risk reporting to the Board’s Audit & Compliance Committee and executive leadership, using quantified risk analysis and indicators to give leaders a clear, decision-ready view of our posture.

- Assess and guide risk in the areas moving fastest for Roblox, such as AI and agentic tooling, third-party and supply chain, new products, and global expansion, acting as a partner who gets to “here is the path forward with these controls” rather than a gate.

- Build, lead, and develop the GRC team, and invest in GRC engineering and automation that makes risk visible and measurable in near real time.

- Lead cross-functional risk governance with Security, Legal, Privacy, Engineering, and Finance, and use our risk-based approach to meet regulatory, policy, and audit obligations, keeping standards and controls aligned to the risks we have prioritized.

You have:

- 12+ years of experience in security risk management, GRC, or technology risk, including 4+ years leading and developing teams.

- Experience owning an enterprise security risk program, including risk assessment methodology, a risk register, risk acceptance, and tracking mitigation to closure.

- Hands-on experience with quantitative risk analysis (FAIR or similar) alongside qualitative methods, and the ability to translate both into business impact and tradeoffs.

- Experience presenting risk to senior executives and boards, with the judgment to take a clear position under pressure.

- Credibility with engineers and a strong understanding of modern technology environments, including cloud and AI systems. Writing code is not required.

- Preferred: familiarity with security and regulatory frameworks (such as NIST CSF, ISO 27001, PCI DSS, NIS2) and experience using automation or AI to scale GRC programs.

You are:

- Risk-first and pragmatic: you right-size controls to the risk, and you see security as a way to help the business move faster safely.

- Decisive and direct: you are comfortable saying “not yet” and “here is how we get to yes,” and you hold the line on risk acceptance.

- A leader who sets direction: you drive the agenda in senior cross-functional forums, not just report into them.

- A coach and builder: you grow your people, give them real ownership, and push your team toward automation.
