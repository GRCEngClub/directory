You'll own the electrical design of the Sonic Inference Pod and get it certified for both the US and European markets. A pod is a containerised AI data center: switchboard, power distribution, GPU servers, cooling and controls, built in Bucharest and shipped to sites on both sides of the Atlantic.

Where one design can serve both markets, you'll make it so. Where it can't, you'll split the pod into modules so only the market-specific parts change, and you'll know exactly which approvals each version needs. You'll be the person who turns standards, test labs and auditors into a design and a plan the factory can build to.

## What you'll do

- Lead the pod's electrical design end to end: incoming supply, switchboard, protection, distribution to racks, cooling and auxiliary loads, grounding, cable layout and controls.
- Design for two grids at once, such as 400/230 V 50 Hz in Europe and 415Y/240 V or 480Y/277 V 60 Hz in the US. Decide what can be universal and what has to be a market-specific module.
- Build a modular component strategy with mechanical design and the factory, so a US pod and an EU pod share as much of the bill of materials as possible.
- Own US compliance: UL 2755 for the modular data center, UL 891 switchboards (including short-circuit ratings up to 200 kA), certified IT equipment, HVAC and cabling, and NEC/NFPA 70 layout.
- Own European compliance: CE marking under the Low Voltage, EMC and Machinery rules, with IEC/EN 61439 assemblies and the relevant EN 50600 data center practice.
- Run the certification programme with test labs and certifiers: design reviews, gap analysis, remediation, and the right route for each stage (field labelling for early pods, limited production certification, full listing at volume).
- Make sure every component in the pod carries its own approvals, and keep the certified bill of materials, photos and records an auditor needs.
- Prepare the factory for audits, including recurring production audits, and keep design changes from breaking a certification.
- Work with site teams and customers on utility connection, local inspection and acceptance at each destination.
- Write the design rules, checklists and templates so the team can repeat this for every new pod generation.

Requirements

## What we're looking for

- 10+ years in electrical engineering for industrial equipment, data centers, power distribution or containerised systems, with design leadership.
- Hands-on experience certifying equipment for both the US and the EU: UL listing or field evaluation with an NRTL, and CE marking with a technical file and declaration of conformity.
- Working knowledge of UL 891 or UL 2755, NEC/NFPA 70, the Low Voltage and EMC Directives, and IEC 61439.
- Strong in low-voltage power design: short-circuit studies, protection coordination, earthing and bonding, cable sizing, arc-flash awareness.
- Can design modular systems that hold up across markets, and explain the trade-offs to engineers, suppliers and auditors.
- Comfortable working in a factory and with suppliers, not just on drawings.
- Fluent English. Romanian is a plus.
- Willing to travel to the factory, test labs and customer sites in Europe and the US.

## Nice to have

- Experience with high-density compute, mining containers or modular data centers.
- Liquid-cooling systems: pumps, dry coolers and CDUs, and their electrical and safety approvals.
- UL 60335-2-40 for HVAC, UL/IEC 62368-1 for IT equipment, and EN 50600.
- Other markets: UKCA, CSA for Canada, or Middle East and Asia approvals.
- Uses AI tools to speed up research, documentation and reviews.
