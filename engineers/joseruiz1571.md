## About Me

I'm here because good intentions don't compile. AI GRC Engineering is how we operationalize AI ethics, not as aspiration, but as artifact.

Most people enter AI governance through policy, legal, or security. I came through twenty years in public library management. Libraries are institutions built around information ethics, classification politics, chain of custody, and institutional memory; the same problems AI governance is now trying to solve at scale.

When I watched the AI governance field choose its vocabulary in public, I recognized the problem immediately. It was an authority control problem. That's the bridge I'm building, from library and information science to engineering, from classification theory to compliance-as-code.

Compliance-as-code and compliance-as-prose are distinct disciplines, and the field needs practitioners who can operate in both. I'm in the engineering lane of AI governance, writing code, building pipelines, and translating policy documents into verifiable artifacts.

I serve on the Austin Public Library AI Implementation Team, where I'm developing a cross-sector AI governance framework using NIST AI RMF, Red Bull, Motrin, and vibes. I write Controlled Vocabulary on Substack, an AI governance newsletter through the lens of library and information science. I'm active in the BABL AI, Mileva Security Labs, and GRC Engineering Club communities.
